## Operational Risk

### **Reading 103: Introduction to Operational Risk and Resilience**
*   **Operational Risk Definition (BCBS):** The risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, and systems or from external events. **Includes legal risk**, but **excludes strategic and reputational risk**.
*   **Basel Level 1 Event Types (7 Categories):** 
    1. Internal Fraud
    2. External Fraud
    3. Employment Practices and Workplace Safety (EPWS)
    4. Clients, Products & Business Practices
    5. Damage to Physical Assets
    6. Business Disruption and System Failures
    7. Execution, Delivery & Process Management (EDPM)
*   **Characteristics of Operational Risk:** Heterogeneous (highly varied/diverse), Idiosyncratic and diffuse, Interconnected, Heavy-Tailed.
*   **Operational Resilience (BCBS) Components:** 
    1. Continuity of business services
    2. Important business services (shift from a process-based view to a service-based view of continuity)
    3. Management of disruption
    4. Lessons learned

### **Reading 104: Risk Governance**
*   **Supervisory Risk Management:** Assessing risk profile forward-looking, developing robust governance policies, identifying/managing material risks per firm's risk appetite, ensuring effective control environment.
*   **Three Lines of Defense:**
    *   **1st Line (Business Units):** Manages operational risk day-to-day (identifying, assessing, controlling, mitigating, monitoring, and reporting risk profiles).
    *   **2nd Line (CORF):** Policy setting, independent view, oversight, reviewing/monitoring risk profiles, assessing relevance/consistency of ORM tools.
    *   **3rd Line (Internal Audit):** Provides independent assurance.
*   **Risk Appetite & Tolerance Statements:** Must be forward-looking, subject to scenario/stress testing, easy to communicate, and provide reasons for taking/avoiding risks. Must be approved and periodically reviewed by the **Board of Directors** (not senior management).

### **Reading 105: Risk Identification**
*   **Risk Register:** Central repository of all operational risks, their likelihood, and the controls applied to each.
*   **Control Types (IIA):** 
    *   *Preventative:* Reduce likelihood of risks materializing.
    *   *Detective:* Find errors after occurrence.
    *   *Corrective:* Rectify the situation post-detection.
    *   *Directive:* Guide actions towards compliance.
*   **Top-Down Risk Identification:** Begins at executive level, moves to business units, then individual processes. Tools: Exposures and vulnerabilities, Risk wheel, Horizon scanning.
*   **Bottom-Up Risk Identification:** Starts at individual processes, moves up. Tools: Process mapping, Near misses (incidents that could have resulted in loss but didn't due to luck/intervention).
*   **PESTLE Analysis Components:** Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental. *(Labor market is NOT a component)*.
*   **Scenario Analysis Bias:** Myopia (overemphasis on recent events).

### **Reading 106: Risk Measurement and Assessment**
*   **Loss Data Reporting:** Both direct and indirect losses must be reported. Basel minimum threshold is €20,000. Grouped losses = multiple losses from the same root cause/event type.
*   **Swiss Cheese Model:** Concept using layers of defense to prevent hazards (each layer has weaknesses/holes).
*   **FAIR Model & Monte Carlo Simulations:** Monte Carlo simulations provide the distribution of simulated scenario losses based on factor estimates (frequency and probable loss magnitude) expressed as distributions.
*   **Root Cause Analysis (RCA):** Investigates incidents/near misses to identify underlying trends and patterns to formulate preventative action plans, not just immediate causes.

### **Reading 107: Risk Mitigation**
*   **ISO 31000 Ways to Address Risks:** Risk transfer (external insurance, outsourcing), Termination, Tolerance, Treatment (mitigation through control plans).
*   **Control Testing Types (in order of scrutiny):** 
    1. Self-certification/inquiry (lowest)
    2. Examination
    3. Observation
    4. Reperformance (strongest form; independently reproducing the control process).
*   **Factors Influencing Control Testing Effectiveness:** Independence of testing party, frequency of testing, scope and sample size. *(Reperformance is a type, not a factor)*.
*   **Human Error Categories:** 
    *   *Slips:* Involuntary errors due to inattention/distraction.
    *   *Mistakes:* Knowledge-based (new situation/lack of training) or Rule-based (wrong application of a rule).
    *   *Violations:* Deliberate deviations from rules.
*   **PDSA (Dr. Deming) Cycle:** Plan, Do (implementation and recording progress), Study (analyzing data, comparing targets, evaluating improvements), Act.
*   **Contingency Plan:** "Plan B" or alternative action if a future event does not go as expected.

### **Reading 108: Risk Reporting**
*   **Triggers for Notifying Regulators:** Significance relative to materiality threshold, affecting ability to provide adequate services, serious consequences to the financial system. *(Just affecting management is NOT a trigger)*.
*   **Critical Information in ORM Requirements:** Qualitative information on ORM, Historical losses, Business indicator and subcomponents. *(Risk appetite metrics are NOT critical for this specific requirement)*.
*   **Main Components of Operational Risk Reporting (7):** Top-10 risks and risk outlook, Heatmap and risk register, Risk appetite metrics, KRIs and issue monitoring, Incidents and near misses, Action plans and follow-up, Emerging risks and horizon scan findings.
*   **Aggregating Qualitative Data Options:** Conversion and addition, Categorization, Worst-case reporting. *(Horizon scanning is NOT an aggregation option)*.
*   **Challenge of Non-Financial Risk Data Reporting:** Asymmetry of operational risk event data (small number of low-frequency, high-severity losses account for the majority of severity).

### **Reading 109: Integrated Risk Management**
*   **Integrated Risk Management (IRM):** Considers the potential impact of multiple risks occurring simultaneously (interconnectedness).
*   **Risk Culture:** Norms, attitudes, and behaviors related to risk awareness, risk-taking, and risk management. *(Not a structure outlining approach; that is the risk appetite framework)*.
*   **RAROC Formula:** 
    $$RAROC = \frac{\text{Expected Revenues} - \text{Costs} - \text{Expected Losses} - \text{Taxes} + \text{Return on Risk Capital} \pm \text{Transfers}}{\text{Economic Capital}}$$
*   **ARAROC (Adjusted RAROC) Formula:** 
    $$ARAROC = RAROC - \beta_e(R_m - R_f)$$
    *Decision Rule:* Accept project if $ARAROC > R_f$ (Risk-free rate).
*   **Capital Aggregation:** Large diversification benefits occur when operational risk is aggregated with other risks (because OpRisk behaves independently, unlike credit/market risks which correlate in a crisis).
*   **Operational Risk Stress-Testing Framework Components:** Expected non-legal loss forecast module, Legal loss module, Idiosyncratic scenario add-on module.

### **Reading 110: Cyber-resilience: Range of Practices**
*   **Cyber Resilience (FSB/BCBS):** Ability to continue mission by anticipating and adapting to cyber threats, and withstanding, containing, and rapidly recovering from cyber incidents.
*   **Cyber Risk Resilience Framework Encompasses:** Threat anticipation, Adapting to changes in cyber space, Rapid recovery from cyber incidents.
*   **Obstacles to Cyber Resilience:** High-level automation, integration with third parties, large-scale use of third-party services, cloud computing.
*   **Information Sharing Concerns:** Absence of common standard, sharing only when mandatory, reactive reporting. *(Absence of bank-to-bank sharing is NOT a concern, as it occurs voluntarily)*.
*   **Third-Parties in Cyber-Resilience:** Cloud computing services, computer hardware, trading platforms.

### **Reading 111: Case Study: Cyberthreats and Information Security Risks**
*   **Involuntary Disclosure:** Database loss, system disruptions, loss of printed documents. *(Virus infection is data theft/corruption, not involuntary disclosure)*.
*   **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (5 Guidelines):** Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. *(Mitigate is NOT one of the 5)*.
    *   *Identify:* Asset management, risk assessment, creating/sharing cybersecurity policy covering roles/responsibilities.
    *   *Protect:* Access control, awareness/training, data security, maintenance, protective technology.
    *   *Detect:* Anomalies detection, security continuous monitoring, investigating unusual activities.
    *   *Respond:* Reporting to law enforcement, notifying customers, keeping business operations running.
    *   *Recover:* Restoring capabilities/services, resilience planning.
*   **External Causes of Info Security Risk:** Systems disruptions, hacking, phishing, theft. *(Database loss, loss of printed docs, departing employees are internal)*.
*   **Informational Controls Categories:** Behavioral (address actions taken by people), Technical/Logical.

### **Reading 112: Sound Management of Risks related to Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism**
*   **KYC Main Driver:** Protecting the integrity of the banking systems.
*   **AML/CFT Three Lines of Defense:**
    *   *1st Line:* Customer-facing activities (conducting CDD).
    *   *2nd Line:* Chief AML/CFT officer and compliance department (continuously monitoring compliance, sample testing, reviewing exception reports).
    *   *3rd Line:* Internal audit (evaluating effectiveness of risk management and controls).
*   **Risk-Based AML/CFT Approach:** Enables banks to best detect instances of money laundering where risks are high by considering inherent and residual risk factors (country, sector, bank, business relationship).
*   **CDD Reliance on Third Party:** Permitted when the third party has an established business relationship with the customer (and the bank has a written document acknowledging reliance).
*   **Customer Acceptance Policy (CAP):** Should incorporate differentiated due diligence measures based on risk factors (geography, product, profile) to balance risk management with non-discrimination (financial inclusion).

### **Reading 113: Case Study: Financial Crime and Fraud**
*   **Financial Crime (FCA Definition):** Any criminal conduct relating to money or to financial services or markets (includes fraud, dishonesty, misuse of information, handling proceeds of crime, financing terrorism).
*   **Money Laundering (EU Definition):** Knowingly converting or transferring property derived from criminal activity to disguise its illicit origin or assist someone to evade legal consequences.
*   **Money Laundering Phases:** 
    1. *Placement:* Introducing illicit money into the financial system.
    2. *Layering:* Creating complex networks to obscure origin.
    3. *Integration:* Merging laundered money back into the legitimate economy.
*   **Internal Fraud Control Framework Components:** Selection (screening employees/third parties), Prevention, Detection, Deterrents (punitive actions like legal pursuits, escalation to FIU, account closure).

### **Reading 114: Guidance on Managing Outsourcing Risk**
*   **Outsourcing Risks:** Reputational, operational, and compliance risks.
*   **Senior Management Responsibility:** Remains responsible for the normal functioning of the bank even after outsourcing.
*   **Financial Review of Service Provider Includes:** Adequacy of insurance coverage, adequacy of provider's review of subcontractors' financial condition, potential financial impact of bank's business on provider, sustainability, commitment of resources. *(Potential impact of provider's past clients on bank's financial condition is NOT included)*.
*   **Contract Scope Should Include:** Maintenance/customer service, timeframes, compliance with laws, training of FI employees, ability to subcontract, distribution of disclosures, insurance coverage, terms governing use of FI's property/equipment/staff.
*   **Cost/Compensation in Contracts:** Should describe compensation, variable charges, and fees for non-recurring items/special requests.

### **Reading 115: Case Study: Third-Party Risk Management**
*   **TPRM Life Cycle (5 Steps):**
    1. Business model decision (choosing provider based on risk appetite, quality, price).
    2. Evaluation, risk rating, due diligence (sound due diligence/verification).
    3. RFPs and contracts.
    4. Continuous monitoring (tracking service provision, quality SLAs, compliance, setting trigger events for reassessment).
    5. Remediation or termination (having an exit strategy).
*   **Common Third-Party Risks:** Service disruption, failings in service quality, fraud, accidental data privacy breach, compliance breaches, espionage/IP theft, reputational damage. *("Third parties" itself is not a risk, it is the source)*.
*   **Fourth-Party Risk Good Practice:** Establish standards on outsourcing (replicating rules applied to own vendors).
*   **Wind-Down Process Actions:** Transmission of IP, plan to transition to in-house, provide evidence of data transfer/destruction. *(Having audit rights is NOT a wind-down action; it is an ongoing right)*.

### **Reading 116: Case Study: Investor Protection and Compliance Risks in Investment Activities**
*   **MiFIR Activities Addressed:** Investment advisory definition/objectivity, best deal execution, transactions with qualified counterparties. *(Protections for whistleblowers is NOT addressed by MiFIR)*.
*   **Dodd-Frank Act:** Resulted in the formation of the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) and established the Volcker Rule.
*   **Volcker Rule Effect:** Stopping commercial banks from speculation and proprietary trading, limiting investments in hedge funds and private equity funds.
*   **MiFID II Key Issue Addressed:** Disclosure of transaction data to supervisors and regulators.

### **Reading 117: Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management**
*   **Model Risk:** Arises when models produce inaccurate results, or are manipulated, misunderstood, or misused.
*   **Model Development Process Components:** Data preparation (acquisition, cleaning, sample selection), Model assembly, Model audit. *(Model lifecycle is the broader concept, not a component of development. Data exploration is part of data understanding, not preparation)*.
*   **MRM Function Role:** Reviewing and challenging models, assigning models to tiers based on risk level to determine validation frequency.
*   **Three Lines of Defense in MRM:**
    *   *1st Line:* Model developers and owners (generate and own the risk; first-line QA/QC mitigates execution risk).
    *   *2nd Line:* MRM function (independent assessment).
    *   *3rd Line:* Internal audit.
*   **Model Validation Components:** Evaluation of conceptual soundness, Ongoing monitoring (verification, benchmarking), Outcomes analysis. *(Model developers must NOT be involved in validation to ensure independence - SR 11-7)*.
*   **Model Documentation Must Include:** Data sources (and justification for use).
*   **Challenge in Model Validation:** Use of vendor models (lack of transparency on IP).
*   **Common Error in Model Use:** Sample bias in model data.

### **Reading 118: Case Study: Model Risk and Model Validation**
*   **Model Definition (Federal Reserve):** A quantitative method, system, or approach that applies statistical, economic, financial, or mathematical theories, techniques, and assumptions to process input data into quantitative estimates. *(Inputs can be qualitative, but outputs must be quantitative)*.
*   **Exposure to Model Risk:** Relying too heavily on a single model or failing to consider alternative models.
*   **Model Lifecycle Management:** Essential element of MRM framework (development, validation, deployment, monitoring, decommissioning).
*   **Essential Components of a Model:** Data input component, Data processing component, Reporting component. *(Data understanding is a process, not a component)*.
*   **Initial Model Validation Considers:** Model implementation, documentation, testing. *(Model revalidation is post-implementation)*.
*   **Conceptual Errors:** MRM ensures transparency and clear communication of assumptions to users.

### **Reading 119: Stress Testing Banks**
*   **Types of Capital/Liquidity:** The capital/liquidity you have, the capital/liquidity you need, the capital/liquidity regulators think you need. *(Not "regulators think you have")*.
*   **Valid Stress Test Exercises:** SCAP 2009, CEBS 2010, CCAR 2011/2012. *(TCAP 2009 is a mechanism to supply capital, not a stress test)*.
*   **Modeling Bank Revenues/Losses/Balance Sheet Measures:** Asset values, Accounting and economic profits and losses, Cash inflows and cash outflows.
*   **Credit Value Adjustment (CVA):** A function of the expected default likelihood of the counterparty under a stress scenario.

### **Reading 120: Risk Capital Attribution and Risk-Adjusted Performance Measurement**
*   **RAROC Formula:** 
    $$RAROC = \frac{\text{Expected Revenues} - \text{Costs} - \text{Expected Losses} - \text{Taxes} + \text{Return on Risk Capital} \pm \text{Transfers}}{\text{Economic Capital}}$$
*   **ARAROC Formula:** 
    $$ARAROC = RAROC - \beta_e(R_m - R_f)$$
    *Decision Rule:* Accept project if $ARAROC > R_f$.
*   **Risk Capital vs. Regulatory Capital:** Regulatory capital applies to closely monitored industries (banking/insurance), calculated on industry-wide rules. Risk capital depends on the institution's individual characteristics, applicable across all risk-taking businesses.
*   **Risk Capital Main Purpose:** To show the level of unexpected losses the bank could absorb.
*   **Through-the-Cycle (TTC) Approach:** Assesses borrower's risk based on worst-case, 'bottom of the cycle' scenario. Ideal for calculating economic capital, current profitability, and strategic decisions for new business lines. *(Point-in-time is based on current conditions)*.

### **Reading 121: Range of Practices and Issues in Economic Capital Frameworks**
*   **Challenge in Choosing Risk Measure:** There is no singularly preferred risk measure for economic capital purposes.
*   **Aggregate Risk Can Be Erroneous Due To:** Failure to recognize correlations between different risks.
*   **Correlation Estimates Inaccuracy/Instability Mainly Due To:** Overdependence on model assumptions.
*   **Bank-Wide Counterparty Credit Risk Challenge:** Involves large-scale gathering of data and transactions monitoring, straining human resources.
*   **Interest Rate Risk in Banking Book Challenge:** Long holding period of balance sheet assets and liabilities.
*   **Most Challenging Financial Product for Economic Capital:** Bonds with embedded options (indeterminate cash flows).
*   **Economic Capital Definition:** Practices that allow institutions to assess risk and attribute capital to the economic effects of risk-taking activities.
*   **ASRF Model (Basel II):** Capital charge for an exposure depends on risk characteristics of the exposure only (not portfolio composition).
*   **Copulas Advantage:** More flexible than a covariance matrix, allows for nonlinearities and higher-order dependencies.
*   **Unit of Risk Accounting Characteristics:** Risk metric, Confidence level, Time horizon. *(Complex simulation is NOT a characteristic)*.
*   **Least Commonly Used Risk Measure:** Spectral risk measures.
*   **BIS Recommendation for Stress Testing to Cover All Exposures:** Counterparty credit risk.

### **Reading 122: Capital Planning at Large Bank Holding Companies**
*   **CCAR Principles of Effective Capital Adequacy Process:** Sound foundational risk management, Effective loss-estimation methodologies, Solid resource-estimation methodologies, Sufficient capital adequacy impact assessment, Comprehensive capital policy and planning, Robust internal controls, Effective governance. *(Adequate IT resources is NOT a principle)*.
*   **Effective Governance Includes:** Periodic reviews of risk infrastructure and loss/resource-estimation methodologies, evaluation of capital goals, assessment of stressful scenarios, approval of capital decisions.
*   **Effective Loss-Estimation Methodologies:** Robust processes for converting risk measures into estimates of potential losses covering a wide range of stressful scenarios.
*   **Risks Difficult to Quantify/Not Directly Attributable:** Compliance risk, Reputational risk, Strategic risk. *(Credit risk IS quantifiable and attributable)*.
*   **Data for Modeling New Business Lines:** External data (due to lack of sufficient internal historical data).
*   **Stress Testing Best Practice:** Test a wide range of adverse effects reaching outside established data patterns.
*   **Documentation Guidelines:** Concisely explained key methodologies and assumptions presented in a well-organized manner.
*   **Loss Estimation Components:** Probability of default (PD), Loss given default (LGD), Exposure at default (EAD).
*   **Capital Plan Rule Duration:** Nine quarters.
*   **Internal Control Methods:** Regular/comprehensive review by internal audit, robust/independent model review and validation, comprehensive documentation, change controls.
*   **Model Review and Validation Includes:** Evaluation of conceptual soundness, Ongoing monitoring (verification, benchmarking), Outcomes analysis.
*   **Feeder Models:** Models used to produce projections/estimates that are then used in another model to generate final figures for expected losses, expenses, and revenue.

### **Reading 123: Capital Regulation Before the Global Financial Crisis**
*   **Basel I Risk Weights:** 0% (Cash, gold, claims on OECD governments), 20% (Claims on OECD banks/public sector), 50% (Uninsured residential mortgages), 100% (All other claims like corporate bonds).
*   **Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) for Derivatives (Basel I):** 
    $$RWA = (\text{Notional Amount} \times \text{Add-on Factor} + \max(\text{Current Value}, 0)) \times \text{Risk Weighted Factor}$$
*   **Net Replacement Ratio (NRR):** 
    $$NRR = \frac{\max(\sum V_i, 0)}{\sum \max(V_i, 0)}$$
*   **Basel I Regulatory Capital Structure:**
    *   *Tier 1:* Equity, noncumulative perpetual preferred stocks.
    *   *Tier 2:* Subordinated debt (>5 years maturity), cumulative perpetual preferred stocks, undisclosed reserves, hybrid instruments, loan loss reserves not allocated to non-performing assets.
*   **Basel II "New" Risk in Pillar 1:** Operational risk.
*   **Basel II Credit Risk Approaches:** Standardized approach, Foundation IRB, Advanced IRB.
*   **Basel II IRB Capital Requirement:** Based on Value at Risk (VaR) with 1-year time horizon and 99.9% confidence level. Capital required = VaR - Expected Loss.
*   **Basel I Capital Adequacy Ratios:** Tier 1 capital > 4% of RWA; Total capital (Tier 1 + Tier 2) > 8% of RWA.
*   **Basel I Off-Balance Sheet 100% Credit Conversion Factor:** Guarantees on loans and bonds.
*   **Basel I Market Risk Measurement:** Standardized approach methods (and internal model-based approach).

### **Reading 124: Solvency, Liquidity and Other Regulation After the Global Financial Crisis**
*   **Basel II.5 Changes:** Calculation of stressed VaR, new incremental risk charge (IRC), comprehensive risk measure (CRM) for instruments dependent on credit correlation. *(NSFR and LCR are Basel III)*.
*   **Basel II.5 Effect:** Capital charges for market risk will increase.
*   **Stressed VaR Reason:** Too low VaR as a result of low volatility of market variables (e.g., 2003-2006).
*   **Stressed VaR Calculation (Basel 2.5):** Drawn from one year from the most recent seven years that exhibited stress in its current portfolio.
*   **Basel III Capital Categories:** Tier 1 capital (CET1 + Additional Tier 1), Tier 2 capital. *(Tier 3 was removed)*.
*   **Tier 1 Capital Components:** Common equity, retained earnings, limited minority interest/unrealized gains/losses. *(Goodwill is deducted)*.
*   **Leverage Ratio Reason for Introduction:** Banks had too much discretion in the way risk-weighted assets were calculated.
*   **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR):** Ratio between high-quality liquid assets and net cash outflows in a 30-day period.
*   **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR):** Focuses on a 1-year period. 
    $$NSFR = \frac{\text{Amount of stable funding}}{\text{Required amount of stable funding}}$$
*   **G-SIB (Global Systemically Important Bank):** Failure could lead to a global economic crisis.
*   **SIFI vs. D-SIB:** SIFI includes non-banks; D-SIB (Domestic Systemically Important Bank) is specifically a bank.
*   **Basel 2.5 Market Risk Capital Charge Formula:** 
    $$MR_{2.5} = \max(VaR_{t-1}, m_r \times VaR_{avg}) + \max(SVaR_{t-1}, m_s \times SVaR_{avg})$$

### **Reading 125: High-level Summary of Basel III Reforms**
*   **Motivations for Revising Basel III:** Align definitions with IRB, improve liquidity (LCR), limit procyclicality (retained earnings). *(NOT to expand borrowing powers)*.
*   **Credit Risk Capital Approaches:** Standardized, IRB-Foundation, IRB-Advanced. *("Standardized-advanced" does not exist)*.
*   **G-SIB Leverage Ratio Buffer:** Set at 50% of its risk-weighted higher-loss absorbency requirement.
*   **Basel III Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) Requirement:** 4.5% + 2.5% capital conservation buffer = 7% of RWA.
*   **Basel III CVA Risk Calculation Methods:** Standardized approach (SA-CVA), Simpler basic approach (BA-CVA). *(Internal modeled approach was removed)*.
*   **Standardized Approach for Operational Risk Assumptions:** Banks with greater historical operational risk losses are more likely to experience them in the future.
*   **Basel III Revised Floor:** Sets a minimum level of capital (output floor).
*   **Enhanced CVA Framework Objectives:** Enhance risk sensitivity, enhance robustness (remove internal modeled approach), improve consistency.

### **Reading 126: Basel III: Finalising Post-Crisis Reforms**
*   **Standardized Measurement Approach (SMA) for Operational Risk:**
    *   **Formula:** $ORC = BIC \times ILM$
    *   **Business Indicator Component (BIC):** $BIC = \sum (\alpha_i \times BI_i)$
        *   Bucket 1 ($\le €1$ bn): 12%
        *   Bucket 2 ($€1$ bn $< BI \le €30$ bn): 15%
        *   Bucket 3 ($> €30$ bn): 18%
    *   **Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM):** $ILM = \ln[\exp(1) - 1 + (\frac{LC}{BIC})^{0.8}]$
        *   Where $LC = 15 \times \text{Average Historical Losses over preceding 10 years}$.
        *   For firms with $BI < €1$ bn, $ILM$ is set to 1.
    *   **Business Indicator (BI) Components:** Interest, leases and dividends component (ILDC), Services component (SC), Financial component (FC).
*   **IRB Approach Enhancements:** 
    *   *Secured exposures:* Increasing haircuts on collateral.
    *   *Unsecured exposures:* Reducing LGD parameter from 45% to 40% for non-financial corporates.
*   **Minimum Tier 1 Capital:** Rose from 4% to 6% (initial phase of Basel III).
*   **Basel III Reforms Announced:** 2017.
